## Figure 1 — Evidence-map workflow

A two-row process flow. An approved protocol and declared source-family pairs
lead to complete open-index exports, normalization and study-family
consolidation, two isolated agent screening passes, separate adjudication,
lawful full-text appraisal with exact locators, bidirectional citation chasing,
accountable-author claim confirmation, and a final evidence matrix, flow ledger,
and bounded novelty conclusion. Citation-network records loop back into the same
workflow. Developmental query records are shown outside the systematic corpus.

## Figure 2 — Hypothesized mechanisms

Demand drivers and risk tier, moderated by stage automation, feed forecast
role-stage demand. Demand combines with capacity and existing queues to produce
capacity pressure and queue delay. Evidence readiness affects gate decisions
and rework. Touch, waiting, blocking, and rework determine verified completion
and cycle time, which precede external quality and UAT criteria. Dashed arrows
indicate hypothesized rather than estimated causal effects.

## Figure 3 — Framework architecture

Three pre-commitment inputs—five demand drivers, stage automation enablement,
and risk-scaled evidence requirements—feed a role-stage service-demand profile.
That profile interacts with effective role capacity, evidence readiness, and
dependencies, queues, handoffs, and bounded rework. Outputs keep touch time,
queue delay, cycle time, completion probability, and unresolved obligations
separate.

## Figure 4 — Lifecycle and roles

An eight-stage snake flow moves from intent and acceptance through architecture
and risk, context and generation planning, implementation, independent review,
integration and QA, release validation, and UAT/business acceptance. Each stage
shows an illustrative accountable role pool. A dashed return path indicates
that evidence failure or conditional risk can trigger bounded rework to an
earlier stage.

## Figure 5 — Simulation and comparator isolation

A development configuration and development-only seed namespace enter a
synthetic discrete-event model with calendars, queues, gates, dependencies, and
rework. The model emits immutable realized outcomes and a separate commitment-
time comparator packet without realized outcomes. These feed Story Point,
HIE-compatible, simple role-load, proposed, and diagnostic-oracle forecasts and
checksummed reports. Locked production seeds and outputs are explicitly outside
the paper route.

## Figure 6 — Comparator result heatmap

A heatmap with eleven developmental scenarios as rows and HIE-compatible,
simple role-load, and proposed comparators as columns. Each cell is the Brier
score difference from Story Points; negative values favor the named comparator.
The strongest negative differences occur for simple role load and the proposed
model in the severe-queue and high-load scenarios. Several baseline scenarios
have near-zero differences, and some high-capacity or low-load cells favor the
Story Point baseline. The figure is labelled developmental synthetic evidence,
not empirical or causal validation.
